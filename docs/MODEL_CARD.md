# Model card: GlucoForecaster (and the GlucoTwin virtual patient)

## Summary
* **Task:** binary forecast, P(glucose > 180 mg/dL at any time in the next 120 min), for forecast times with current glucose ≤ 160 mg/dL. Secondary regressor for expected 2 h peak.
* **Model:** gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting), isotonic calibration on validation patients, alert threshold chosen on validation patients for about 80% sensitivity.
* **Inputs:** 44 features (16 EHR, 16 CGM history, 4 activity, 2 HRV, 4 sleep, 2 time-of-day), computed from data available at forecast time only (a unit test checks there is no future leakage).
* **Training data:** fully synthetic (150 virtual patients, 14 days, 5-minute resolution, generated by `glucotwin/synthetic.py`).
* **Intended use:** research and education; demonstration of EHR + wearable fusion in a digital twin.
* **Not intended for:** diagnosis, treatment decisions, insulin dosing or any clinical use. No regulatory clearance.

## Evaluation
**Synthetic held-out patients** (patient-level 70/15/15 splits, mean ± sd over 3 splits)

| Model | AUROC | AUPRC |
|---|---|---|
| EHR only | 0.674 ± 0.014 | 0.279 ± 0.015 |
| CGM only | 0.942 ± 0.004 | 0.768 ± 0.022 |
| Fused EHR + wearables | 0.942 ± 0.004 | 0.775 ± 0.024 |
| Cold start, wearables only | 0.935 ± 0.006 | 0.739 ± 0.032 |
| Cold start, fused | 0.940 ± 0.005 | 0.762 ± 0.025 |

These labels come from our own simulator, so these numbers measure how well the pipeline learns a known process, not real-world accuracy.

**Real-data check (CGMacros v1.0.0, PhysioNet, open access)**
45 adults (15 healthy, 16 prediabetes, 14 T2D), about 10 days each, Dexcom G6 Pro, 25,306 eligible forecast times, event prevalence 12.1%. Only CGM-derived and time-of-day features can be used (no HRV, sleep or steps in this dataset). 95% bootstrap CIs resample whole participants.

| Setting | AUROC (95% CI) | AUPRC |
|---|---|---|
| A. Trained on synthetic only, tested on real | 0.826 (0.795-0.853) | 0.422 |
| B. Trained and tested on real (participant-grouped CV) | 0.827 (0.795-0.852) | 0.454 |
| B + 7 shared EHR fields | 0.817 (0.783-0.842) | 0.446 |
| A, T2D participants only (n=14) | 0.835 (0.811-0.856) | 0.554 |
| B, T2D participants only (n=14) | 0.820 (0.799-0.835) | 0.577 |

* AUROC falls from about 0.94 (synthetic) to about 0.83 (real): a clear sim-to-real gap.
* A synthetic-only model transferred about as well as one trained on the small real set.
* EHR fusion gave no measurable gain on real data. The fusion benefit is shown only in simulation.
* With Libre Pro readings instead, AUROC was about 0.89 to 0.90 and prevalence 5.4%. Libre values may be interpolated, so Dexcom (chosen in advance) is the primary result.

**Alert burden** (60-minute cool-down; fraction of excursion episodes warned in advance)

| Alerts per patient-day | Real (cross-validated) | Synthetic test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 31.5% | 26.9% |
| 2 | 50.5% | 56.8% |
| 3 | 62.1% | 70.1% |
| 5 | 79.4% | 94.6% |

The original operating point (about 5.8 alerts per patient-day) warned 97.9% of episodes in simulation. Thresholds were picked on the same predictions they are reported on, so these figures are illustrative.

## Digital-twin components
* **Forecast:** the model above, plus per-forecast driver explanations (each feature group replaced by a typical-patient value).
* **What-if:** mechanistic simulator re-run with paired meals and noise.
* **IR calibration (`VirtualPatient.calibrate_ir`):** insulin resistance is estimated from the first 5 days of CGM by grid search, using different seeds from the observed data and matching summary statistics only. On 150 synthetic patients: correlation 0.88, MAE 0.094 (0.164 when predicting the cohort mean). In 5 patients checked, the estimated twin underestimated the benefit of a post-meal walk. Because the same simulator generates and fits the data, this is optimistic and has not been tested on real patients.
* **Still true values in what-if:** walk habit, resting heart rate, meal timings and the noise seed.

## Known limitations
* Trained only on synthetic data from a simplified physiology simulator. Real-data validation covers 45 adults at one US site, mostly mild T2D, and is not clinical evidence.
* Performance depended on the CGM sensor (Dexcom vs Libre).
* HRV, sleep and steps add little predictive value in the simulation, and could not be tested on real data.
* About 6 alerts per patient-day at the original threshold; a realistic budget of about 2 per day warns roughly half of the episodes.
* Fixed 180 mg/dL threshold; operating points were not chosen on separate data.
* No subgroup fairness analysis is possible with synthetic demographics, and the real cohort is too small for one.
* What-if results reflect simulator assumptions, not clinical evidence. Calibration was tested only in simulation.

## Risks if misused
Alert fatigue, false reassurance, over-reliance on simulated counterfactuals, applying a model trained on simulated data to patients unlike the validation cohort.

## Next steps
* Larger and more varied real-data validation (for example ShanghaiT2DM, subject to its licence), under ethics approval for any clinical data.
* Estimate more twin parameters (walk habit, meal timing) from observed data, and test calibration on real CGM.
* Operating threshold chosen on separate data, with uncertainty intervals.
* Clinician-tuned alert policy (tolerable alerts per day, two-tier alerting).
* Regulatory pathway assessment.
